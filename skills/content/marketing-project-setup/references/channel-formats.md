# Channel formats

A profile's format list tells the AI which shapes of post have actually worked
for people this audience follows. Build it from evidence, not from generic
"content ideas".

## Extracting formats from top performers

1. Ask the user for 3 to 5 creators their audience already follows, and for
   their top posts. Outlier posts (far above that creator's own median views)
   show what works better than raw view counts do. The user may paste them or
   point to a tool that ranks outliers.
2. For each post, record the hook (first line), the structure, the kind of
   evidence (revenue, cost, latency, counts) and what readers did (bookmarks,
   replies, views).
3. Group posts into formats. Write each format as a pattern with a placeholder
   example, never as a copied post.
4. Add common traits across creators, then the source and the date collected.

## Starting catalog for X

Patterns seen across indie hacker and builder accounts on X. Confirm them
against the user's own sources before relying on them.

**Common traits**
- The first line is the whole hook: a number, a claim or a discovery. No warm-up.
- First person, real story, specific numbers; failures told in numbers too.
- Practical posts (open repos, rules, tools) collect the most bookmarks.
  Strong opinions and personal stories collect the most views.
- No hashtags. Emoji work as labels (one per product line), rarely as decoration.

**Formats**
1. **Monthly revenue report:** "I made $[X] in [month]", one line per product
   with its number, margin and a one-line lesson.
2. **Milestone with real lessons:** a big number, the path, failures in numbers,
   one short lesson.
3. **Open source on demand:** "[N] people asked for [X], so I open-sourced it",
   with features and the install command.
4. **Launch:** what it is and what it does, details in the next post. A rebuild
   framed by a number ("[N]% cheaper") also works.
5. **One-line opinion:** a single line with a clear stance.
6. **Myth-bust:** quote what people say, refute it, show the better way.
7. **Specific discovery with numbers:** "[Change], [metric] went from [A] to [B]."
8. **Cost transparency:** an itemized cost table, then ask the audience what
   they would do.
9. **Public audit:** analyze the numbers of a well-known product.
10. **Comparison or quiz:** "[A] vs [B]" or "Guess which converts better?
    Answer in the next post."
11. **Confession:** a real struggle, ending with "anyone else?".
12. **Life outside work:** travel, observations, family. Makes the account a
    person and reaches a wider audience.

Other channels: run the extraction steps above on that channel's top
performers instead of reusing the X list.
